# History's Last Hours: 100-day operating plan

Day 0 is 2026-10-01; Day 100 is 2027-01-09. The goal is 100,000 subscribers and millions of engaged views. These are targets, not a forecast. This plan starts from the 2026-10-03 owned-channel read: one public Short, eight channel views, zero subscribers, and no useful feed-retention sample.

## Now: repair and relaunch

- Keep the channel scheduling hold while inaccurate or mismatched hosted drafts remain. Withdrawn public versions stay private. Rebuild the Winton public Short and the held R101, Yamaguchi, New Fire, Peshtigo, Chicago, Galveston, Empire State, Little Bighorn and Alamo drafts against checked sources.
- For each candidate, retain a claim map, image identity and rights receipt, final media hash, full decode/audio/speech report, full-video review and independent QA outcome. A corrected local render is a draft until those checks pass.
- Make a four-Short pilot batch from different angles: Winton rescue, R101 survival, Yamaguchi survivor, and Chicago legend. Use an event-specific first frame and at least one meaningful visual change, such as a route map, document reveal or timeline. Release only reviewed candidates, at most one per day, with a day between the first few pilots when practical.
- Verify the exact Buffer asset and due time before every post, then verify the owned YouTube ID, visibility and description after it goes live. Keep the cloud workflow and watchdog active; a stopped provider must surface an alert rather than silently reuse old media.

## Days 7–21: learn from qualified traffic

| Check | Decision |
|---|---|
| 24 and 72 hours after each public pilot | Record engaged views, viewed versus swiped away, average viewed percentage, subscribers gained, traffic source and first retention drop when available. |
| After 10 comparable Shorts with Shorts-feed traffic | Compare rescue, survivor, warning and myth-correction angles; compare archive-first openings with explanatory map openings. Keep the strongest verified treatment. |
| If the first second loses viewers | Rewrite the first line and frame on the next candidate; do not merely add louder effects or a bigger claim. |
| If people view but do not subscribe | Make the series promise clear in the last line and channel page; test an adjacent story in the winning series. |
| If distribution is too small to compare | Continue reviewed pilots and log the absence of evidence. Do not call a topic or time slot a failure. |

## Days 22–100: scale what holds attention

- Aim for a steady reviewed supply before increasing cadence. Start at no more than one release a day; test two only when the prior batch is stable and the QA queue can sustain it. Do not generate a backlog of unreviewed uploads to meet a count.
- Within 48 hours of a real breakout, research a related follow-up from a different documented detail. Preserve source and image quality.
- Around Day 30, review the packaging and visual language if no Short has meaningful feed traffic. Around Day 45, test a short documentary compilation or a deeper 2–4 minute story from the best sourced series. These are creative tests, not automatic uploads.
- Each week, audit one published Short against its source receipt and platform description, inspect any comments that identify a possible error, and compare same-age retention and subscriber conversion by series. Correct material mistakes promptly and log every privacy or replacement action.
- Recheck YouTube Studio's monetization eligibility and policy notices monthly. Shorts views and subscribers can grow without producing immediate income; the first revenue path depends on eligibility, review and the audience's location.

## Operating dashboard

The weekly report should show: reviewed candidates, rejected candidates and reasons, scheduled posts with exact hashes and local/US Eastern due times, public IDs and visibility, cloud run health, source or visual corrections, engaged views per eligible Short, viewed-versus-swiped share, average viewed percentage, subscribers per 1,000 engaged views, and the next experiment. Use `analytics/` and the owned YouTube API for measured data; clearly label unavailable fields.
