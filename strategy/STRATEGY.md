# Atlas in Numbers: editorial and growth strategy

Updated 2026-10-08, when channel 2 was rebuilt from History's Last Hours as Atlas in Numbers. The research behind
the switch is in `Desktop/YouTube/channel-review-2026-10-08/niche-research.md`; how the lane runs is in `CHANNEL.md`.
The History's Last Hours strategy is kept at the end for its lessons only.

## Why the channel changed

History's Last Hours had 4 public Shorts and 37 views after 112 attempts. 68 drafts were rejected, most because
each tragedy needed event-specific, openly licensed period pictures and the image check could not find them. The private control repo that signed and published Shorts also ran out of GitHub
Actions billing minutes, so nothing could ship. Tragedy topics also get limited ads. On 2026-10-08 the four public
History Shorts were made private and the lanes were retired.

## Editorial promise

One world map, one number. Every country is coloured by one official dataset (World Bank WDI, CC BY 4.0), the camera
tours the surprising countries, one plain line says why, and the last line loops back to the hook. Every number on
screen or in the narration comes from the dataset snapshot kept beside the script (`data.json`). English, for a
US audience; India is always drawn in its official borders. Never war, elections, religion, ethnicity, disputed
territory, health or financial advice, or breaking news.

Every frame is drawn from data the studio is licensed to use, so no topic can be blocked by a missing picture.

## Format

- **Length:** 30–45 seconds, 75–120 words, 6–8 beats, 1080×1920.
- **First two seconds:** the claim and a fully coloured map on frame 1, with captions burned in (many viewers watch
  muted).
- **Body:** what the colour means (metric, year, source), then a zoom tour of 3 or so surprising countries, one
  sentence each, then the "why".
- **End:** a question that invites "where does my country rank?" comments, and a source card (dataset, year,
  licence) that loops back to the hook.
- **Description:** data credit, Natural Earth credit, and the AI and synthetic-voice disclosure (`atlas/publish`).
- **Later sub-formats** (only after the base format has data): Rank Ladder, Versus split map, Odd One Out,
  Then→Now. Vary palette and camera path between them so videos never feel interchangeable.

## Publishing and learning

- Three Shorts a day at 12:00, 16:00 and 19:00 ET (CHANNEL.md, How it runs). Volume plus sameness is the main
  trigger for YouTube's inauthentic-content review, so vary topic family, shot types (the rank bars) and palette, and
  drop to two a day if the 24-hour medians fall as volume rises.
- At 24 and 72 hours record views, viewed versus swiped away, average viewed percentage, shares, comments and
  subscribers per 1,000 views in `LEARNINGS.md`. Record missing metrics as missing.
- Don't change the format until about 20 Shorts have 24+ hours of data. Then compare topic families (people,
  money, nature, technology) and hook patterns ("Only N countries…", "This map explains…", "India is #1 here…").
- Crossposting to Facebook and Instagram Reels and a weekly long-form "deep map" come after the first 30 days,
  and only if the Shorts are being tested by the feed (some Shorts above 1,000 views).
- No views, subscribers or earnings are promised. Check YPP eligibility in YouTube Studio.

## Before 2026-10-08: History's Last Hours

The release contract (claim-level citations, matched visuals, hash receipts, independent sign-off) was sound, but it
needed a supply of pictures the niche could not provide. Keep the lessons: pick niches whose visuals the studio
can generate or license in bulk; a publishing path must not depend on a billed service that can silently stop; and
judge a niche on feed data, not on a handful of views.
