# Creature Receipts: strategy

Last revised 2026-09-30 (Day -3). Evidence for each choice comes from this channel's research in
`research/channel/`: niche fit, picture depth, and policy in
`research/channel/pipeline-fit-and-policy.md`; market, competitors, and subscriber conversion in
`research/channel/niche-market.md`; the 2026 Shorts growth evidence and the 15 pipeline upgrades in
`research/channel/growth-playbook-2026.md`. The kit's general Shorts research stays in `research/`
(`research/shorts-strategy.md`, `research/policy-factsheet.md`).

## Goal and honest odds

- **Owner's goals:** YouTube Partner Program (YPP) approval on Shorts as fast as possible, and as many
  subscribers as possible by Day 100. Day 0 is 2026-10-03 and Day 100 is 2027-01-11.
- **What YPP takes:** 1,000 subscribers plus 10 million engaged Shorts views in 90 days. From 2027-02-01 new
  applicants need 20 million (`research/channel/pipeline-fit-and-policy.md`, section 6). Review takes about a month
  and YouTube hasn't said which bar applies to applications still pending on Feb 1, so we apply the day Studio
  shows eligibility, aiming for mid-December (about Day 75) and never later than Jan 31. 10M by mid-December
  means averaging about 130,000 engaged views a day from launch.
- **Engaged views, not views:** since 2025 the public count includes replays, and on Aug 24, 2026 every format
  moved to first-frame counting. YPP, earnings, and recommendations run on engaged views, which exclude loops
  (`research/channel/growth-playbook-2026.md`, bottom line 2). Every view target in `strategy/PLAN-100.md` is in
  engaged views; loops are for watch time and satisfaction, not for YPP arithmetic.
- **What subscribers take:** animal-fact Shorts convert poorly. The two fastest newcomers in the genre earn
  1.3 (doggyzuko, 303K subscribers from 60 Shorts) and 0.9 (Infinite Fact, 142K) subscribers per 1,000 views
  (`research/channel/niche-market.md`, section 3), below the 1.5–3.6 of general Shorts
  (`research/shorts-strategy.md`, section 6). Stories and series convert better than fact feeds, which is why
  every Short here is a sourced story in a named series. Helix², a faceless anatomy channel, reached 238K
  subscribers from 54 Shorts on a fresh, repeatable concept (`research/channel/growth-playbook-2026.md`, section 4):
  breakouts, not volume, make fast channels.
- **Our honest cases** (full table in `strategy/PLAN-100.md`):
  - **Base:** 10M engaged views and about 12,000 subscribers by Day 100. The 10M-in-90-days bar is met around
    Day 100, so YPP comes by applying in January, before the Feb 1 change.
  - **Stretch:** 40M+ engaged views and 50,000 subscribers, with YPP applied for in late November. It needs
    a Short a week passing 1M views from about Day 30.
  - **Floor:** if no Short passes 50,000 views by Day 30, the format changes before any volume is added.
- **Plan:** a steady stream of well-made, sourced attempts; measure each against the channel's own numbers;
  put volume behind what works (follow-ups within 48 hours of a breakout). Never use tactics that put the
  channel at risk (Hard rules in `CHANNEL.md`, and "Never" below).

## Niche and audience

- **Niche:** true, surprising stories of strange animals, extreme biology, and deep-sea life. One story or
  fact per Short, and every claim carries "receipts": at least two reputable sites behind it, listed in the
  description. Promise: one true, jaw-dropping animal story in under a minute, with receipts.
- **Why animals** (`research/channel/pipeline-fit-and-policy.md`):
  - **Best fit for this pipeline of 12 niches measured:** a median of 99 usable (public domain, CC0, or CC BY)
    Commons files per topic with none under 89, the richest Wikipedia articles (median 73 KB and 140
    references, enough for eight two-source claims), and the most reader demand (2,572 daily article views
    per topic; section 2). Every beat gets a different, real picture of the right species, the thing the
    quality gate checks most.
  - **Deep sea included on purpose:** deep-ocean creatures are well photographed (NOAA Ocean Exploration
    images are public domain), but deep-sea places and abstract topics are thin (Challenger Deep: 5 usable
    files). Folding the deep sea into animals keeps the creatures and drops the places (section 2). The
    audience is big: 72K people search the topic monthly and one "why deep sea creatures are giant" Short passed
    42M views (`research/channel/niche-market.md`, section 3).
  - **More pictures:** iNaturalist's research-grade CC0 and CC BY photos (2048 px originals, no key) are
    wired into the picker, and `pick.MODERN_BEFORE` is 0 so modern color photos are never penalized
    (`research/channel/pipeline-fit-and-policy.md`, section 4). The Biodiversity Heritage Library's
    public-domain plates cover extinct and rarely seen animals.
  - **Low policy risk:** "non-graphic depictions of animal violence in the natural world" can earn full ads;
    ads are limited only when graphic injuries are the focus (`research/channel/pipeline-fit-and-policy.md`,
    section 5b). No health, finance, legal, or political advice.
  - **Evergreen with spikes:** the axolotl or the Greenland shark keeps getting views for months, and new
    species, rediscoveries, and record observations give dated hooks.
  - **Revenue, honestly:** animal RPMs are low ($1.5–4 long-form, $0.02–0.04 per 1,000 Shorts views;
    `research/channel/niche-market.md`, section 3). This channel is built for YPP and subscribers first; long-form
    compilations are the revenue hedge (Growth lever 6).
  - **The honest weakness:** "animal facts" is the most crowded Shorts genre, scoring 1 of 5 on competition
    (`research/channel/niche-market.md`, section 3). The angle that separates us is stories with receipts:
    named people, dates, specimens, and records, sourced and told precisely, instead of an unsourced list of
    facts over stock clips.
  - **Not a sister channel's niche:** space and astronomy belong to Universe Receipts, and strange-but-true
    human history to the kit's first channel. No space topics or animals-in-space angles here.
- **Audience:** English-speaking adults 18–44, mostly the US, then the UK, Canada, and Australia; curious
  generalists more than zoologists, plus teachers and nature-documentary fans.
- **Not doing:** long-form for now (Shorts only, compilations later), Hindi, cryptids or monsters presented
  as real, "nature is metal" gore, animal cruelty or distress, pet care or handling advice, AI renderings of
  real animals, present-day politics.

## Format

- **Length:** 40–58 seconds (105–135 spoken words, 6–9 beats; `ytc check` enforces 35–58 s). vidIQ's July 2026
  study of 331M Shorts found 45–59 s gets the highest average views (`research/channel/growth-playbook-2026.md`,
  bottom line 3); staying under 60 s keeps us clear of the rule that blocks longer Shorts with a copyright claim.
- **Five rotating structures** (`writer.STRUCTURES`; the writer is handed whichever the last 8 episodes used
  least, so no two in a row share a shape): `story`, `myth_vs_fact`, `scale_ladder`, `mystery`, and `record`.
  Each opens inside the story with a hook of 12 words or fewer and ends on a line that loops back into it.
  Full rules in `strategy/SCRIPT-RULES.md`.
- **Hook styles** (`writer.HOOK_STYLES`: statement, question, number, contradiction; every hook must be
  literally true):

| Style | Example |
|---|---|
| Statement | "This fish was supposed to be extinct for 66 million years." |
| Question | "Why does a wood frog's heart stop every winter?" |
| Number | "400 years. That's how old this shark might be." |
| Contradiction | "The platypus lays eggs, and it makes milk." |

  Series give each style its flavor: a record ("No mammal dives deeper than this whale"), a myth flip
  ("Goldfish don't have a three-second memory"), a rediscovery ("This insect vanished in 1920, then turned up
  on a sea stack").
  "Scientists just found" is used only for a finding dated within about a week, from a museum, agency, or
  peer-reviewed paper.
- **Hook on screen from frame 0:** the hook's picture and its 2–6-word on-screen text are fully visible in the
  first frame, with no fade-in (`captions.py`; `research/channel/growth-playbook-2026.md`, upgrade 2). The
  swipe-or-stay decision is made in the first second.
- **Loop seam:** the last line is a complete sentence that echoes the hook, and the tail after the last word is
  trimmed so the replay feels like the next line (upgrade 3).
- **Visuals:** real photos of the exact species (iNaturalist, Commons, NOAA, USFWS), then museum specimens and
  fossils, then public-domain natural history plates, then diagrams and range maps, and designed title cards
  (a date, a number, a quote) when no picture fits. Never dead, injured, or distressed animals, gore, cartoons,
  or AI renderings of real animals (`strategy/SCRIPT-RULES.md`, Visual rules).
- **Captions:** burned in, 2–3 words at a time, white with the current word highlighted, inside the Shorts
  safe area.
- **Voice:** Kokoro-82M `af_heart` (Apache-2.0), a warm American narrator, with a blind test against a male
  voice (`am_michael`) in the first weeks. A generic synthetic narrator that impersonates no one needs no AI
  disclosure (`research/channel/pipeline-fit-and-policy.md`, section 5c). The owner's own voice is the strongest
  originality signal and the fallback if YouTube ever questions the channel.
- **Audio:** narration at -14 LUFS with synthesized whooshes, pops, and risers. No licensed music.

## Series

Named series make the channel look curated rather than templated and give viewers a reason to follow; they
are also the unit YouTube's new Shorts Series feature plays in order once we're in YPP
(`research/channel/growth-playbook-2026.md`, bottom line 5 and upgrade 12).

| Series | Promise |
|---|---|
| Built Different | Real bodies that sound impossible: regrowing limbs, surviving freezing, punching like a bullet |
| Deep Sea Files | The strangest life in the deep ocean, and how we found it |
| Back From Extinction | Animals declared gone that turned up alive, and the people who found them |
| Evolution Got Weird | The odd, clumsy, brilliant results of evolution, and what they tell us |
| Nature's Record Breakers | The fastest, oldest, deepest, and longest, with the real numbers |
| Animal Myths, Busted | Things almost everyone believes about animals, shown wrong with receipts |

Each series has its playlist (filled by `ytc playlists`) and visible episode numbers. After Day 21, output
shifts toward the series with the best engaged views per Short and subscribers per 1,000 views.

## Publishing

- **Cadence** (`auto.PACE`): 2 Shorts a day from Day 0, 3 from Day 15, 4 from Day 30, and 5 from Day 50.
  Each step past 2 is earned: the last 10 Shorts must keep at least 90% of both the median engaged views per
  Short and the median engaged share of the 10 before them; without 20 Shorts of numbers the pace stops at 3.
  If engaged views per Short fall 30% below the previous 10 (`PACE_DROP`), the pace drops back to 2. This is
  upgrade 4 of `research/channel/growth-playbook-2026.md`: the 2026 consensus for new channels is 1–3 a day, and
  a channel that went from 3 to 5 a day reported collapsing from 75K daily views to near zero. Volume only helps
  while each Short keeps its audience. Never more than 5 a day. Production keeps up to two weeks of finished
  Shorts ready (42).
- **Times (US Eastern; `publish.SLOTS`, in the order they're added):** 7:00 p.m., 9:30 p.m., 12:30 p.m.,
  4:30 p.m., and 10:30 p.m. Buffer's 2026 study of 1.8M videos found Shorts peak 6–11 p.m. local, with
  Friday 4–7 p.m. the best slots (`research/channel/growth-playbook-2026.md`, section 4 and upgrade 5), so the
  first two slots sit in the evening peak.
  - Until Oct 31 (US daylight time): 4:30 a.m., 7:00 a.m., 10:00 p.m., 2:00 a.m., and 8:00 a.m. IST.
  - From Nov 1 (US standard time): 5:30 a.m., 8:00 a.m., 11:00 p.m., 3:00 a.m., and 9:00 a.m. IST.
  - After 28 days, check Studio's "When your viewers are on YouTube" report and move slots only with evidence.
- **How:** Buffer's free plan (an official YouTube API partner) publishes from a queue of up to 10 posts,
  kept full by the cloud studio; the rest wait on disk, so a missed run never breaks the schedule.
- **Packaging:**
  - **Title:** 60 characters or fewer, accurate curiosity, with the animal's name in about the first 40
    characters, since search and Google's Shorts carousel match on it (`research/channel/growth-playbook-2026.md`,
    section 5). Plain claims work best ("This Shark Can Live 400 Years"). At most one word in capitals, no
    emojis, and the Short delivers exactly what the title says.
  - **First frame:** the most striking real photo of the animal with the on-screen hook. Shorts are chosen
    in the feed by the first frame, not a thumbnail.
  - **Description:** one or two sentences naming the animal in the first, then the sources ("receipts") and
    image credits, which the pipeline appends.
  - **Hashtags:** exactly 3: `#animals` plus two specific ones (`#axolotl`, `#deepsea`, `#sharks`).
  - **Settings:** category Pets & Animals or Education (whatever the channel's upload defaults set), not
    made for kids (the audience is adults, and "made for kids" disables comments and personalized ads), AI
    disclosure only if a Short ever contains realistic AI imagery (none is planned).
- **Anniversaries:** dated stories go out at 7 p.m. Eastern on the date (`strategy/CALENDAR.md`): Winnipeg the
  bear (Oct 3), Cher Ami (Oct 4), Paul the Octopus (Oct 26), the vampire bat for Halloween, the megamouth
  shark (Nov 15), *On the Origin of Species* (Nov 24), and the Thanksgiving turkey pardon (Nov 26).

## Originality safeguards (YPP review)

YouTube's July 2025 policy, clarified in July 2026, demonetizes channels with too much generic, repetitive,
or template-based content, unsatisfying or off-putting content, or AI personas giving advice, and it named
"narrated stories and slideshows with only superficial differences" as mass-produced
(`research/channel/growth-playbook-2026.md`, bottom line 6; `research/channel/pipeline-fit-and-policy.md`, section 5a).
Reviewers judge the whole channel. For an automated channel the existential risk is looking templated, not
using AI. Every Short must be clearly original:

- Each script is written from reputable sources, with a claims table in its folder: two independent sites
  behind every claim, and the sources listed in the description.
- **Sameness check** (`writer.sameness`; upgrade 6): a script is rewritten if it shares a quarter or more of
  its three-word phrases with any of the last 40 scripts, or opens with the same four words as one of them.
- Five structures and four hook styles in rotation, six series, and a different real picture for every beat;
  no two Shorts share a storyline.
- Real, credited photos of real animals, not AI slop. "Off-putting" content is avoided by design: no gore,
  no distress, no shock framing.
- Project files (research, script, render manifest) are kept for every Short to show in any appeal;
  winning appeals showed scripts and timelines.
- No reused footage from other creators and no readings of text we didn't write.

## Growth levers, in order of expected impact

1. **Hooks and first frames:** the first second decides "viewed vs swiped away". There is no official
   threshold; YouTube's advice is to compare a Short with your own Shorts of similar length, so the studio
   scores each Short against the channel's median (`research/channel/growth-playbook-2026.md`, bottom line 1 and
   upgrade 1). Hook styles are tested weekly.
2. **Topic demand:** well-known animals stop more thumbs. The studio takes each series' most-read Wikipedia
   topic first (`auto.demand`), so the axolotl, the octopus, and the Greenland shark go before obscure
   species; the calendar's 114 topics have a median of about 12,500 article views in 30 days. The twist is
   always the least-known true detail, so a famous animal still surprises.
3. **Breakout follow-through:** when a Short reaches 3x the median of the channel's Shorts at the same age
   within 48 hours (at least 1,000 views), or 100,000 views outright (`growth.BREAKOUT_*`), the studio queues
   follow-ups on the same animal or angle (`auto.FOLLOW_UPS`). The owner pins a comment and sets its Related
   video to the next episode.
4. **Series binge:** series playlists with episode numbers, endings that tease the next episode, and the
   Related video link, the one clickable bridge in Shorts (`research/channel/growth-playbook-2026.md`, section 5
   and upgrade 12). Shorts Series once in YPP.
5. **Volume with quality:** about 290 Shorts by Day 100 if the pace holds at 3, up to about 410 if every step
   triggers, all through the quality gate. Every reviewed Short is another chance at a breakout.
6. **Long-form compilations (from about Day 45):** a weekly 8–12-minute episode built from one series' best
   Shorts with new narration, context, and connecting material, never a plain stitch-up
   (`research/channel/growth-playbook-2026.md`, upgrade 7). It adds watch hours (the 4,000-hour path is a hedge
   against the 20M Shorts bar), higher long-form RPM, and a Related video target.
7. **Cross-posting (optional):** native, watermark-free uploads of the same masters to Instagram Reels and
   TikTok, and Facebook through Meta's own sharing, within the limits of Buffer's free plan (upgrade 14).
   Starts once the owner creates those accounts.
8. **Collaborations:** YouTube's Collab feature lets channels share a Short and reach all their audiences.
   Approach small nature and science creators once we have a few hits.
9. **Community:** reply to real comments, and turn good viewer questions ("what's the oldest animal alive?")
   into episodes.

## KPIs

Healthy levels are relative to the channel's own median, per YouTube's own guidance; the absolute numbers are
early benchmarks, not official thresholds.

| Metric | Healthy | Action if below |
|---|---|---|
| Engaged share (engaged views / views, the API stand-in for "stayed to watch") | At or above the channel median; 60%+ early benchmark | Rework hooks and first frames |
| Engaged views per Short (7-day median) | Rising week over week; 1,000+ by Day 30 | Change topics or formats; the pace gate holds or drops volume by itself |
| Average percentage viewed | 80%+ (loops can pass 100%) | Tighten scripts, cut slow beats, fix the loop seam |
| Subscribers per 1,000 engaged views | 1.3+ (the genre's best newcomer) | Strengthen series identity and endings |
| Likes per 100 views | 4+ | Better payoffs |
| Shorts over 100k views | At least 1 by Day 45 for the stretch case | Breakout follow-through, hook experiments |
| Engaged views in the last 90 days | On pace for 10M by mid-December | Decide the YPP path (`strategy/PLAN-100.md`) |

## Weekly experiments

One variable at a time, judged on at least 10 Shorts per arm:

- Hook style: statement vs question vs number vs contradiction.
- Length: about 42 vs about 55 seconds (and later a 60–90 s arm for `story` Shorts; upgrade 9).
- On-screen hook text: a number ("400 YEARS OLD") vs the stakes ("NO HEART FOR MONTHS").
- First frame: a close-up of the animal vs the animal in its habitat.
- Voice: `af_heart` vs `am_michael` vs the voice-test winner.
- Title style: plain claim ("This Frog Freezes Solid Every Winter") vs a question vs a myth flip.
- Subscribe line: a specific, series-based ask vs none (upgrade 13).
- Posting time: only after the 28-day audience report.

## Promotion rules

Allowed: native cross-posts to Reels, TikTok, and Facebook; collaborations with other nature and science
creators; genuine participation in communities such as r/Awwducational, r/marinebiology, r/biology, and
r/natureismetal (the last only with non-graphic posts, and never gore), following each subreddit's rules,
with self-promotion under 10% and never asking for votes; replies to comments; asking viewers to subscribe.

Never:

- Bought views, subscribers, likes, or comments; bots; hired promoters who do any of this (their violations
  count against us).
- Sub-for-sub, engagement pods, rewarded engagement ("comment to win"), comment spam, spammy polls.
- Clickbait lies: a title, hook, or first frame the Short doesn't deliver; "scientists are terrified", fake
  "just found" for old news, cryptids presented as real, danger or doom the sources don't state.
- Shock content: gore, kills, injured or distressed animals, cruelty, even when it would get views.
- AI slop: AI renderings passed off as real animals, templated Shorts with no real research, or readings of
  someone else's text.
- Reuploads or narration over other creators' footage (documentary clips, viral animal videos, TikToks).

## Risks and mitigations

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Rejected for "inauthentic content" | Sourced original scripts, sameness check, five structures and six series, real credited photos, saved project files, owner voice as fallback |
| Lost in the crowded "animal facts" genre | Stories with named people, dates, and specimens; receipts in every description; famous animals with their least-known true twist; series identity |
| A factual error goes viral (biologists comment) | Two-source rule per claim, estimates kept as estimates, records stated as the sources state them, quick correction in a pinned comment |
| Wrong species pictured | Species-exact `subjects` and queries, iNaturalist taxon photos, SigLIP ranking plus a second-look check; the reviewer rejects look-alikes |
| Limited ads or "off-putting" flag | No dead, injured, or distressed animals; predation stated once, plainly; the picker vetoes gore |
| Copyright claim on an image | Only public domain, CC0, or CC BY (never CC BY-SA, NC, MBARI, or aquarium press images), credits in every description |
| Miss the Feb 1, 2027 YPP change | Apply the day Studio shows eligibility; track the 90-day pace from Day 45; decide the path at Day 60 (`strategy/PLAN-100.md`) |
| Views plateau or fall with volume | The pace gate holds or drops volume by itself; weekly experiments; series reweighting at Day 21; format change at Day 30 if the median is under 1,000 |
| Buffer, Cloudinary, or model outage | Up to two weeks of finished Shorts, a queue of 10 in Buffer, `slot_watch` resends, manual Studio upload as last resort |
