## Channel description (paste as is)

```text
True animal stories that sound made up, in under a minute, and every claim comes with receipts.

The peacock mantis shrimp throws a punch at about 51 miles per hour. The coelacanth, a fish scientists thought died out with the dinosaurs, was pulled alive from the sea off South Africa in 1938. History's Last Hours tells the true stories of strange animals, extreme biology, and deep-sea life, and backs every one with proof.

Our series:
- Built Different
- Deep Sea Files
- Back From Extinction
- Evolution Got Weird
- Nature's Record Breakers
- Animal Myths, Busted

Every Short is researched from peer-reviewed studies, NOAA, museums, and field scientists, and its sources are listed in its description. Check the receipts yourself.

Subscribe for your daily dose of the wild. For space, visit our sister channel, Universe Receipts.

Channel art: NOAA public-domain photos (humpback whale, dumbo octopus) and a peacock mantis shrimp by prilfish (CC BY 2.0).
```

## Channel trailer idea (about 45 seconds, vertical)

1. **Cold open (0 to 3 s):** Black screen, marine snow drifting. Text: "This fish was supposed to be extinct." Cut to a
   coelacanth.
2. **The receipt (3 to 10 s):** A white receipt prints on screen, line by line: "Coelacanth. Presumed extinct: about 66
   million years. Found alive: 1938, South Africa." The lime bar stamps "VERIFIED."
3. **Rapid fire (10 to 35 s):** Three more receipts, each on real footage: a peacock mantis shrimp strike ("Punch speed:
   about 51 mph"), a Greenland shark ("Lifespan: at least 272 years"), and a dumbo octopus ("Home: the deep sea").
   Each receipt ends with its source, for example "Source: Patek et al., Nature, 2004."
4. **The promise (35 to 42 s):** Voiceover: "True animal stories that sound made up. Every one with receipts."
   The six series names tear off the receipt one by one.
5. **Close (42 to 45 s):** The avatar fills the screen: "History's Last Hours. Subscribe." Small line: "Sister channel of
   Universe Receipts."

## Voice of the channel

Amazed, clear, and exact. We tell the story like a friend who just read something incredible, and we never
exaggerate beyond the sources. Every number has a source, estimates are called estimates, and animals are never
mocked or put in danger for a shot. No gore for its own sake.
