# Private pilot research: Quebec Bridge, 1907

Researched 2026-10-04 for History's Last Hours. This is a **script and visual proposal**, not an approved episode, release-batch change, calendar entry, or permission to schedule. The channel-wide scheduling hold remains active.

## Editorial angle

The documented surprise is the Royal Commission's distinction between saving the bridge and saving lives. On August 27, a bend in a lower chord was found to have grown and was measured. The bridge fell on August 29. The commission concluded that action after August 27 probably could not have prevented the fall, but that better judgment by those in charge might have prevented the deaths. Tell this as a structural warning and a decision under uncertainty. Do not invent a shouted warning, an ignored stop order, a single villain, or a fix that would have saved the structure.

## Proposed 30-second narration (American English)

> On August 27, 1907, a steel chord in the unfinished Quebec Bridge had bent further. The bridge team measured it. Two days later, the bridge collapsed over the St. Lawrence River. The Royal Commission found the failed lower chords' design defective. It did not believe the bridge could be saved after August 27. But better judgment, it said, might have prevented the loss of life.

This is a draft. An independent review estimates about 30–32 seconds with pauses; read it aloud and time the exact take before locking the edit. Keep the commission's qualifiers (`did not believe`, `might`) in the final narration. A precise death toll is omitted because it is unnecessary to this short.

## Claim ledger

| Narration claim | Directly inspected support | Precision / limit |
|---|---|---|
| The unfinished Quebec Bridge had a growing bend in a steel chord on August 27, 1907. | [Royal Commission report OCR](https://archive.org/download/reportandplansa01schngoog/reportandplansa01schngoog_djvu.txt), [original report scan, printed p. 86 / PDF p. 93](https://archive.org/download/reportandplansa01schngoog/reportandplansa01schngoog.pdf#page=93), describing chord 9-L of the anchor arm: “The bend ... was discovered about 9.30 a.m., August 27, to have greatly increased, it having been previously noted and being under observation.” | This was an observed deformation, not proof that inspectors knew the precise failure mechanism. Avoid describing a crack or a broken beam. |
| The team measured the deflection. | Same report, printed p. 86 (scan PDF p. 93), Mr. Kinloch's testimony: “This measurement, which was made by Birks, McLure and myself, showed the extent of the deflections.” | “Bridge team” is safer than assigning a measurement to a named person in a very short script. |
| The bridge collapsed on August 29, 1907, over the St. Lawrence near Quebec. | The report's commission mandate names the collapse in the course of construction over the St. Lawrence River on August 29, 1907. | An image from the **second** collapse in 1916 must not illustrate this beat. |
| The failed lower chords had a defective design. | [Commission's finding (a), printed p. 9 / PDF p. 16](https://archive.org/download/reportandplansa01schngoog/reportandplansa01schngoog.pdf#page=16): “The collapse ... resulted from the failure of the lower chords in the anchor arm near the main pier. The failure of these chords was due to their defective design.” It says the stresses were expected during normal erection, not abnormal weather. | The report also assigns other design and oversight errors. Do not reduce the entire disaster to a single bent member or person. |
| After August 27, the commission did not believe the fall could be prevented; loss of life might have been prevented. | [Commission findings (i) and (j), printed p. 9 / PDF p. 16](https://archive.org/download/reportandplansa01schngoog/reportandplansa01schngoog.pdf#page=16): “We do not believe that the fall of the bridge could have been prevented by any action that might have been taken after August 27, 1907”; “The loss of life on August 29, 1907, might have been prevented by the exercise of better judgment on the part of those in responsible charge of the work.” | Attribute this conclusion to the commission. “Might” is essential: the report does not prove a specific evacuation order would have saved every worker. |

## Proposed visual sequence and rights records

1. **0–4 s: before photograph.** [McCord Stewart Museum item MP-0000.1154.3](https://collections.musee-mccord-stewart.ca/en/objects/140309/le-pont-de-quebec-avant-le-desastre-quebec-qc-1907) is a Neurdein Frères collotype explicitly titled “Quebec Bridge before the disaster,” dated 1907. The item marks the image Public Domain, including commercial copying and modification. Frame the whole print in 9:16. The collection site returned 403 here; its [read-only item text](https://r.jina.ai/http://collections.musee-mccord-stewart.ca/en/objects/140309/le-pont-de-quebec-avant-le-desastre-quebec-qc-1907) was inspected, but an image file at suitable resolution still needs to be obtained and visually checked. The earlier [British Library view No. 48](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Quebec_bridge_view_No_48_(HS85-10-18817).jpg) remains a fallback, with its pre-collapse date unestablished.
2. **4–12 s: explanatory structural drawing.** Draw a deliberately simplified side elevation labeled `SCHEMATIC`: south anchor arm, south main pier and lower chord. The commission names A-9-L/A-9-R near the main pier, but its cited drawings 5, 28–30 and 36 are absent from the inspected text scan. Indicate only an *approximate landward lower-chord area* and animate a dated measurement mark; do not draw an exact bent panel, curvature or marker over a photograph. [Library and Archives Canada catalogs 37 technical drawings](https://recherche-collection-search.bac-lac.gc.ca/eng/home/record?idnumber=4142711&app=FonAndCol&resource=folderlist), but its page was challenge-blocked here.
3. **12–20 s: after photograph.** [McCord Stewart Museum item MP-0000.1154.9](https://collections.musee-mccord-stewart.ca/en/objects/140312/le-pont-de-quebec-apres-le-desastre-quebec-qc-1907) is the matching Neurdein Frères collotype titled “Quebec Bridge after the disaster,” dated 1907. Its [read-only item text](https://r.jina.ai/http://collections.musee-mccord-stewart.ca/en/objects/140312/le-pont-de-quebec-apres-le-desastre-quebec-qc-1907) marks it Public Domain with commercial modification allowed. A [740 × 435 Commons copy](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Pont_de_Quebec_1907.jpg) was visually inspected and is available; retain print margins rather than enlarging the low-resolution wreckage. A [second wreck file on Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Quebec_Bridge_Collapse_of_1907.jpg) is only 403 × 303 and its claimed “Commission report Appendix 19, figure 20” provenance could not be matched to the inspected scan; exclude it until that claim is resolved.
4. **20–end: finding in the original record.** Show a short, legible excerpt of findings (i) and (j) from the report, with the word `might` retained. A restrained document reveal and highlight carries the conclusion more honestly than simulated collapse footage.

**Excluded lookalike:** [File:Quebec Bridge Collapse.jpg](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Quebec_Bridge_Collapse.jpg) is explicitly dated **11 September 1916**, the bridge's second collapse. Its description also contains a questionable 1907 death toll. Do not use it in this 1907 episode.

The museum's [current copyright FAQ](https://www.musee-mccord-stewart.ca/en/collections/photographic-services-and-copyright/) says public-domain images may be copied, modified and used commercially; credit the artist and museum with each accession number even though it is not required. Its [older generic terms](https://www.musee-mccord-stewart.ca/en/collections/photographic-services-and-copyright/copyright-terms-and-conditions/) contain noncommercial and no-modification wording. Preserve the item-level Public Domain records and resolve that conflict before public release.
