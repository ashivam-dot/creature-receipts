## Direct source and rights receipts

For Peshtigo, the [Wisconsin State Climatology Office](https://climatology.nelson.wisc.edu/wisconsin-peshtigo-fire/) returned HTTP 200 and states 1,200–2,500 deaths, warm and dry fall conditions, strong southwest winds, smoldering fires, a firestorm, the town's consumption in under two hours and two buildings left. Its page itself identifies the opening lithograph as a September 1871 before-fire view. The [Peshtigo Fire Museum's Pernin account](https://peshtigofiremuseum.com/fire/chapter-3/) returned HTTP 200 and quotes his time in the river; it also says Chicago burned the same night. The [NWS-hosted Green Bay Press-Gazette retrospective](https://www.weather.gov/grb/peshtigofire) returned HTTP 200 and says the Chicago Fire still “seems to hog the headlines”; that is a republished newspaper observation, not NWS climate research. Commons imageinfo listed the four selected prints as public domain: the town lithograph 5208 × 4248, Peshtigo fire engraving 650 × 423, river refuge print 1024 × 768, and Chicago engraving 3312 × 4096. The event engravings are artistic depictions.

For Chicago, [The O'Leary Legend](https://greatchicagofire.org/oleary-legend/) directly documents her bed testimony, no proof from the inquiry, Ahern's *boast* and disputed authorship. [The Great Conflagration](https://greatchicagofire.org/great-conflagration/) places the start in or near the barn; [The Ruined City](https://greatchicagofire.org/ruined-city/) says “One hundred thousand Chicagoans lost their homes.” All returned HTTP 200. The [1997 City Council journal](https://cdn-web-main.bibliocms.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/3/2014/09/counciljournal10281997.pdf) returned HTTP 200 and SHA-256 `562b0e77866baa004f5a144a9688685ae3dadaa76ba50588021b57e9acd39377`. Its scanned pp. 54829–54830 show 47 yeas, no nays and adopted words forever exonerating O'Leary and her cow. The linked Chicago Public Library page returned HTTP 202 with no body from this environment; the primary journal was readable.

The former Chicago opening [Commons image](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Mrs_OLeary%27s_cow.jpg) claims public domain but its metadata calls the proposed *Harper's Magazine* 1871 source dubious and points to Bettmann/Getty. The replacement [NYPL catalog item](https://digitalcollections.nypl.org/items/8e370600-c53b-012f-ff21-58d385a7bc34) names Copelin & Hine and says NYPL believes it is public domain in the United States. NYPL dates issue to 1871; the pictured card itself bears an 1872 copyright notice. The new package avoids an exact art year. Commons lists the remaining Currier & Ives blaze image as 1871/public domain, the SMU courthouse stereograph as 1871/no restrictions, and the NYPL scan as public domain. Image credits appear in the generated description; no Getty credit remains.
