# ep090 research: Why Did a Wealthy Texas School Use Free Waste Gas? ⚠️ (The Last Hours)

Researched 2026-10-06 by the cloud studio (gemini-3.5-flash, gemini-3.5-flash-lite, gemma-4-31b-it) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: On March 18, 1937, the New London School in Texas—one of the wealthiest schools in America, built with oil boom riches—suddenly evaporated in a massive explosion. To save three hundred dollars a month, school trustees had bypassed commercial natural gas to tap into a local residue gas line. Because untreated natural gas is completely odorless, a slow leak under the school went unnoticed, completely filling the crawlspace. Just minutes before the final bell, manual training instructor Lemmie R. Butler flipped the switch on an electric sander. A tiny spark ignited the trapped gas, instantly launching the building’s roof and crushing hundreds of students and teachers. The tragedy claimed nearly three hundred lives. In the aftermath, a devastated public demanded action. The disaster directly inspired the Texas Engineering Practice Act and laws mandating that natural gas be mixed with a distinctively smelly chemical, mercaptan, ensuring gas leaks could never go unnoticed again.

## Disputed or left out

- The exact death toll: S1 lists 425 killed and a later estimate of 294, S6 lists 298, S2 and S3 say nearly/almost 300, and S4 says about 300. The script should say 'nearly 300 children and teachers died' to remain general and uncontroversial.
- The exact time of the explosion: S2 states the explosion occurred at 3:05 p.m., while S1, S6, and S7 place it at 3:17 p.m. The script should generalize this as 'just after 3 p.m.' or 'minutes before the final bell.'
- The precise text on the recovered blackboard: S2 reports the board read 'Oil and natural gas are East Texas’ greatest natural gifts...' while S6 reports 'Oil and natural gas are East Texas’ greatest mineral blessing.' The script should attribute this as 'a blackboard found in the ruins praised oil and gas as the region's greatest blessing.'
