# ep008 research: Cher Ami's True WWI Mission (Animal Myths, Busted)

Researched 2026-09-30 by the cloud studio (gemini-3.5-flash-lite, gemma-4-31b-it) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: During World War I on October 4, 1918, the American Lost Battalion was trapped in France's Argonne Forest and accidentally bombarded by friendly fire. Major Charles Whittlesey wrote a desperate plea for help and had pigeon handler Omer Richards release Cher Ami, a British-bred homing pigeon. As the bird took flight, German troops opened fire. Cher Ami was shot through the breast and had a leg nearly severed by a bullet or shrapnel, leaving the message tube hanging by a single tendon. Miraculously, the wounded bird took to the sky again, flying 25 miles back to headquarters in just 25 minutes. His flight stopped the friendly fire and helped save the 194 surviving men. Though popular legend claims he lost an eye and flew on October 4, official records and modern DNA analysis reveal he was biologically male and might actually have been wounded later in October.

## Disputed or left out

- Whether Cher Ami actually flew for the Lost Battalion on October 4 or was wounded on October 21/27 from Grandpré. The script should attribute the Lost Battalion flight as popular legend or official Army promotion: "according to popular legend" or "historians note official records point to a different date."
- Whether Cher Ami was blinded in one eye. Photographs from 1919 show no evidence of an eye injury, so the script should leave out the eye injury claim.
