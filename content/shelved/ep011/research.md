# ep011 research: The 66-Million-Year-Old Fish That Came Back (Back From Extinction)

Researched 2026-09-30 by the cloud studio (gemini-3-flash-preview, gemini-3.5-flash-lite, gemma-4-31b-it) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: On December 22, 1938, museum curator Marjorie Courtenay-Latimer spotted a strange blue fin in a fisherman's catch in South Africa. She had found a coelacanth, a creature thought to have gone extinct 66 million years ago with the dinosaurs. This 'Lazarus' fish was unlike anything modern science had seen. It lacks a true backbone, possessing instead a pressurized, oil-filled tube called a notochord. It has a hinged skull that allows its mouth to swing open like a trapdoor and a 'rostral organ' in its snout that detects electrical signals from prey. Scientists later discovered that these survivors are more closely related to humans and land animals than to 99% of other fish. They are deep-sea ghosts that live for 100 years, perform vertical headstands in the current, and endure a staggering five-year pregnancy before giving birth to live pups.

## Disputed or left out

- The exact gestation period: S7 cites 14 months while S1 and S5 cite 5 years based on more recent research; the script should attribute the 5-year figure to recent studies.
- The closest relative to tetrapods: While all sources agree coelacanths are closely related to tetrapods, S2 and S4 clarify that lungfish are actually the closest living relatives.
