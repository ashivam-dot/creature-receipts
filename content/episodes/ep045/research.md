# ep045 Winton private replacement receipts — 2026-10-03

The earlier upload `v4rp0oWvgSI` was made private on 2026-10-03 after its survival-count claim exceeded the cited evidence; see `withdrawal.json`. Its earlier `public_clarification.json` remains as a historical record. This replacement is a local draft. The old opening (“Many believe”) had no documented audience belief to rebut; the old ending overstated secrecy and Grete Winton's knowledge. The old Stockholm arrivals photograph depicted children from Nazi Germany arriving in Sweden, not Winton's Prague-to-Britain group. The old `Pol5.jpg` showed a Polish 7TP tank, not a German invading tank. Both off-event images are removed.

## Direct institutional and family sources

| Claim in the replacement | Direct receipt |
|---|---|
| 669 rescued with help from Winton and a team | [United States Holocaust Memorial Museum](https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/article/nicholas-winton-and-the-rescue-of-children-from-czechoslovakia-1938-1939) says 664 appear in the scrapbook and five more were identified, yielding the official 669. It details Winton's work with other people. The [Nicholas Winton family exhibition](https://www.nicholaswinton.com/exhibition/kindertransport) names Trevor Chadwick and other Prague and London volunteers and describes eight transports bringing 669 children. |
| Largest planned transport for 250, due 1 September 1939, cancelled with the invasion of Poland | The [family exhibition](https://www.nicholaswinton.com/exhibition/kindertransport) states the 250-child transport was organized for that date and cancelled by German authorities when Germany invaded Poland and borders closed. [BBC News's 2009 retrospective](https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/uk_news/8227929.stm) also gives the date and 250 count. Its phrase “on board” is not repeated: boarding is not established by the family source. |
| Scrapbook brought the rescue into public view nearly fifty years later | [USHMM](https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/article/nicholas-winton-and-the-rescue-of-children-from-czechoslovakia-1938-1939) says Grete found the scrapbook in 1988. The [family's recognition account](https://www.nicholaswinton.com/exhibition/recognition-1988) says Winton sought a home for it in 1987; Dr Elizabeth Maxwell helped bring the story into public view in 1988. The replacement names the public result without claiming a 50-year secret from his wife. |
| Memorial at Prague station | [Commons file record](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Praha_hlavn%C3%AD_n%C3%A1dra%C5%BE%C3%AD,_Winton%C5%AFv_pomn%C3%ADk_a_zavazadlov%C3%BD_voz%C3%ADk_(01).jpg) identifies the photographed Flor Kent Winton memorial at Prague Main Station, photographed in 2015. This is a retrospective monument, not a 1939 scene. |

The replacement does not assert that all 250 boarded or assign a precise wartime survival count. USHMM's overview does not quantify this uncompleted transport. The family exhibition says nearly all are *thought* to have been sent to camps, but this remains outside the narration.

## Visual provenance and first seconds

- The first frame is a cropped, verified [1938 Nicholas Winton portrait](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Nicholas_Winton_(1909-2015)_in_1938_by_the_Associated_Press.jpg) with “TRAIN NEVER LEFT” above it. The crop keeps Winton and avoids implying that the pictured child was one of the 250.
- The 669 figure and the 1 September invasion are designed fact/date cards. Neither uses stock children or a tank.
- The Prague Main Station location view is a [1931 Walter Mittelholzer photograph](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Praha_hlavn%C3%AD_n%C3%A1dra%C5%BE%C3%AD_1931.jpg) with “PRAGUE · 1931 PHOTO” burned into a local derivative `assets/prague-station-1931-labeled.jpg`. It does not purport to show the cancelled train.
- The final memorial is an explicitly retrospective [2015 image](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Praha_hlavn%C3%AD_n%C3%A1dra%C5%BE%C3%AD,_Winton%C5%AFv_pomn%C3%ADk_a_zavazadlov%C3%BD_voz%C3%ADk_(01).jpg), credited to ŠJů under CC BY 4.0 and cropped to show the complete statue. The source URL and dates are in `short.yaml`; the description labels all historical and retrospective images.
