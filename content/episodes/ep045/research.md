# ep045 research: Why Nicholas Winton’s Largest Rescue Train Never Left 💔 (The Last Hours)

Researched 2026-10-02 by the cloud studio (gemini-3-flash-preview, gemini-3.5-flash, gemini-3.5-flash-lite, gemini-3.7-flash) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: In December 1938, a 29-year-old British stockbroker named Nicholas Winton canceled a Swiss ski holiday. Prompted by a call from a friend, he traveled to Prague and witnessed thousands of refugees fleeing Nazi expansion. Alarmed by the threat, Winton bypassed bureaucracy to rescue unaccompanied children. Working tirelessly, he secured British foster homes and raised the required £50 guarantees for each child.

Between March and August 1939, Winton successfully organized eight trains, saving 669 mostly Jewish children. But his largest transport of 250 children was scheduled for September 1, 1939. As the children sat waiting on the Prague platform, German troops invaded Poland. The borders slammed shut, and the train never departed. Almost all 250 children aboard were sent to concentration camps; only two survived.

Winton never told anyone of his heroic deeds. Fifty years later, his wife found a dusty scrapbook in their attic, finally revealing a savior who had quietly preserved generations.

## Disputed or left out

- The exact departure date of the ninth train is disputed: S3 states it was scheduled for September 3, 1939, while S1, S2, S4, S6, and S7 state it was September 1, 1939. The script should use September 1, 1939, as the invasion date halted the transport.
- The exact year Winton was knighted is disputed: S3 states 2002, while S5 and S6 state 2003. The script should refer generally to him being knighted in the early 2000s without specifying the exact year.
- Whether the records were found in a briefcase or a scrapbook is disputed: S3 says a briefcase in the attic, while S1, S5, S6, and S7 say a scrapbook in the attic. The script should refer to a scrapbook, as it is the most agreed-upon term.
