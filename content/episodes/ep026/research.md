Story: On the night of October 8, 1871, a booming Wisconsin lumber town called Peshtigo vanished in less than two hours. Following a severe summer drought and rampant land-clearing slash fires, an eastward-moving cold front brought hurricane-force winds that turned small fires into a monstrous firestorm. Generating temperatures of 2,000 degrees Fahrenheit and fierce updrafts, the blaze vaporized entire forests and caused buildings to literally explode. Hundreds of desperate residents plunged into the frigid Peshtigo River to escape the wall of flame, while others succumbed to smoke, drowning, or hypothermia. Between 1,200 and 2,500 people lost their lives, making it the deadliest wildfire in American history. Yet because the Great Chicago Fire erupted four hundred miles south on that exact same night, the apocalyptic inferno at Peshtigo was cast into historical shadow and largely forgotten.

## Disputed or left out

- The exact death toll varies between estimates of 1,200 and 2,500 due to destroyed local records; scripts should attribute this as a range according to historical estimates.
- A long-running speculation suggests the fires were ignited by fragments of Biela's Comet, but modern science and experts dispute this as unproven legend or hypothesis; scripts should present it only as speculation or leave it out.
