# ep013 research: Scientists killed the oldest clam by accident (Nature's Record Breakers)

Researched 2026-09-30 by the cloud studio (gemini-3-flash-preview, gemini-3.5-flash-lite, gemma-4-31b-it) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: In 2006, researchers from Bangor University were dredging the seafloor off Northern Iceland for a climate study. They collected an ordinary-looking ocean quahog clam and threw it in the freezer with the rest of their samples. They didn't know they had just killed a world record holder. Only after they sliced the shell open to count its growth rings did the truth emerge: the clam, nicknamed 'Ming,' had been alive since 1499. Born during the Ming Dynasty, it had survived 507 years on the cold ocean floor, outlasting the Renaissance, the Industrial Revolution, and both World Wars. Initially, scientists miscounted and thought it was 405, but revised the age in 2013 using carbon-14 dating and cross-matching patterns. While the discovery provided a 500-year 'tape recording' of ocean temperatures, the irony remains: Ming only became a household name because scientists accidentally ended its five-century-long life to study it.

## Disputed or left out

- The method of death: While some early reports suggest opening the shell killed it, later sources clarify it was frozen to death on the ship prior to any analysis. The script should emphasize it died during the collection/preservation process.
- Sex of the clam: One Icelandic name 'Hafrún' is female, but researchers officially recorded its reproductive state as 'spent' and its sex as unknown.
