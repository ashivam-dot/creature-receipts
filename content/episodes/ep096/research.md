Researched 2026-10-06 by the cloud studio (gemini-3.5-flash-lite, gemma-4-31b-it) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: For centuries, historians believed that the wealthy Roman resort town of Herculaneum escaped the AD 79 eruption of Mount Vesuvius entirely. Because early excavations revealed almost no human remains, scholars assumed every inhabitant had successfully fled. That comforting narrative shattered in the 1980s when archaeologists excavated the ancient shoreline and found hundreds of skeletons huddled inside the vaulted stone boat houses. Trapped by the rising tide and blocked by the raging sea, about 300 desperate residents had waited for rescue boats that never arrived. At around 1 am on August 25, a superheated pyroclastic surge rushed down the mountain at 100 mph, plunging into the town with temperatures exceeding 900 degrees Fahrenheit. The intense thermal shock caused instant death, vaporizing soft tissue and leaving behind families frozen in their final moments, forever altering our understanding of history's most infamous volcanic disaster.

## Disputed or left out

- The exact month of the eruption is debated; traditional accounts point to August 24, but autumn indicators like heavy clothing, fresh produce, and sealed wine jars suggest October or November. The script should attribute autumn details to recent archaeological findings.
