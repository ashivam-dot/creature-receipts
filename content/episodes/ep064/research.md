# ep064 research: How Did Mud Trap French Knights at Agincourt? (The Last Hours)

Researched 2026-10-04 by the cloud studio (Gemini) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: On October 25, 1415, a starving, disease-ridden English army under King Henry V faced annihilation in northern France. Outnumbered and trapped, the English withdrew to a narrow field of recently ploughed land hemmed in by dense forests. Heavy rain had turned the soil into a swamp of thick, knee-deep mud. The French launched a massive cavalry and armored infantry charge, but it was a disaster. Under a terrifying hail of longbow arrows, the heavily armored French knights sank into the muck. The crush of their own advancing ranks pushed them together so tightly they could not even swing their swords. Many suffocated under the weight of their own falling comrades or drowned inside their helmets. Sensing a desperate opportunity, lightly equipped English archers dropped their bows and slaughtered the trapped knights with hammers and axes. In the tragic chaos, Henry ordered the execution of thousands of prisoners before claiming a miraculous, legendary victory.

## Disputed or left out

- The exact sizes of the English and French armies (ranging from traditional accounts of 25,000 French vs 5,700 English, to modern administrative revisions suggesting 12,000 French vs 9,000 English).
- Whether the French boasted that they would cut off the fingers of captured English archers.
- The exact number of English casualties, which contemporary accounts put as low as double digits, but other records state between 112 and 1,600.
- The precise location of the battlefield (traditional site in the defile between Azincourt and Tramecourt versus theories suggesting a field west of Azincourt).
