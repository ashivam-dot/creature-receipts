Story: On October 8, 1871, a devastating blaze broke out on DeKoven Street in Chicago. Driven by severe drought and high winds, the Great Chicago Fire destroyed thousands of buildings, killed nearly 300 people, and left 100,000 homeless. Looking for a simple culprit, reporter Michael Ahern published a story claiming an Irish immigrant woman named Catherine O'Leary started the disaster when her cow kicked over a lantern in her barn. Rooted in widespread anti-Irish prejudice, the nation eagerly embraced the narrative. In reality, Catherine O'Leary was asleep in bed when the fire started, and the official investigation found no proof of her guilt. Decades later, Ahern publicly admitted he had fabricated the cow story for colorful news copy. Harassed and shamed, O'Leary spent her remaining years as a recluse until her death in 1895. It took until 1997 for the Chicago City Council to formally exonerate her.

## Disputed or left out

- The exact year and circumstances of reporter Michael Ahern's confession vary across sources (cited as 1893, 1911, or 1921, with some claiming it was ghostwritten) — script should attribute as 'according to later accounts' or leave out specific dates.
- The true origin of the fire is unproven (theories include neighbor Daniel Peg Leg Sullivan, men gambling in the barn, or a meteor shower) — script should state that the official cause remains undetermined.
- The name of O'Leary's cow (Daisy, Madeline, or Gwendolyn) is folklore — script should leave it out or label as legend.
