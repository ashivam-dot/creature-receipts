# ep035 research: Why Did the US Ignore the 1900 Galveston Hurricane? ⚠️ (The Last Hours)

Researched 2026-10-01 by the cloud studio (gemini-3-flash-preview, gemini-3.5-flash-lite) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: In 1900, Galveston was the 'Jewel of Texas,' a booming metropolis of Victorian mansions. Local weather chief Isaac Cline had publicly declared it 'impossible' for a major hurricane to strike the low-lying island. This complacency proved fatal. While world-class Jesuit meteorologists in Cuba tracked a monster heading for Texas, US Weather Bureau director Willis Moore blocked their reports out of professional jealousy and banned the word 'hurricane' to avoid 'panic.' On September 8, the Category 4 storm struck an unprepared city. A 15-foot surge swallowed the island, which peaked at only 8.7 feet in elevation. Survivors described a 'wall of debris' crushing everything in its path. At an orphanage, nuns tied children to their waists with clotheslines; they were found the next day, still tethered and drowned. With 8,000 dead, survivors were forced to burn bodies in massive beach pyres. Galveston survived, but its golden era was swept away forever.

## Disputed or left out

- Isaac Cline's heroic beach patrol: According to his own accounts, he warned thousands of people, but some historians find no survivor corroboration and argue his earlier dismissal of a seawall caused the high death toll.
- Exact death toll: Sources cite ranges between 6,000 and 12,000; the script should note that 8,000 is the most commonly accepted official figure.
