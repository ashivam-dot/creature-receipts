# ep106 research: Why Did the Sky Turn Black on a Sunday in 1935? ⚠️ (Warnings Ignored)

Researched 2026-10-07 by the cloud studio (gemini-3-flash-preview, gemini-3.5-flash-lite, gemini-3.7-flash) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: On April 14, 1935, a day known as 'Black Sunday,' the Southern Plains experienced the most severe 'black blizzard' in American history. The morning dawned clear and warm, leading residents to believe the years of drought were easing. However, a massive cold front from Canada soon collided with warm air, whipping up a wall of dust that displaced 300,000 tons of topsoil. As the storm approached, the only warning was the crackle of static electricity and flocks of birds fleeing in terror. Day instantly became night; the dust was so thick that visibility dropped to where people could not see their own hands. Families huddled in darkness, hanging wet sheets over doorways and rubbing Vaseline in their nostrils to keep from suffocating. This catastrophic event, documented by a 22-year-old Woody Guthrie, permanently named the region the 'Dust Bowl' and forced millions to flee their buried farms for California.
