# ep050 research: Why Did a 1910 Idaho Ranger Draw His Gun? ⚠️ (Doomed Expeditions)

Researched 2026-10-03 by the cloud studio (gemini-3.5-flash, gemini-3.5-flash-lite, gemini-3.8-flash) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: In the dry summer of 1910, the newly established U.S. Forest Service faced thousands of small wildfires raging across the Northern Rockies. Then, on August 20, the nightmare began. Hurricane-force winds slammed into the mountains, whipping the scattered blazes into a single, three-million-acre monster. The firestorm incinerated entire towns in minutes, sending flaming trees flying through the air like spears. Darkness fell by midday as a colossal smoke shroud blanked the nation, blinding ships hundreds of miles out in the Pacific. Trapped in the inferno, Forest Ranger Ed Pulaski made a desperate gamble, leading 45 panicked firefighters into an abandoned mine tunnel. Holding them at gunpoint to prevent them from fleeing into the flames, Pulaski held a wet blanket over the entrance until he collapsed. All but five survived. The tragedy claimed at least 85 lives and sparked a century of controversial total fire suppression.

## Disputed or left out

- The exact total death toll of the fire is disputed among sources, ranging between 85, 86, and 87. The script should state that between 85 and 87 people died.
- The claim that Pulaski drew his gun to prevent his men from fleeing is disputed because it relies primarily on his own later account. The script should attribute this detail with "according to Pulaski" or "as the story goes".
- The exact location where the 28-man crew perished is named as Setzer Creek in S1 and S7, but Storm Creek in S6. The script should use Setzer Creek or omit the specific creek's name.
