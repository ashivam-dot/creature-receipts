# ep070 research: How Did an 1888 Spring Morning Freeze New York? ❄️ (The Last Hours)

Researched 2026-10-05 by the cloud studio (gemini-3-flash-preview, gemini-3.5-flash, gemini-3.5-flash-lite, gemini-3.6-flash, gemini-3.8-flash) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: On March 11, 1888, commuters in New York stepped into unseasonably mild, spring-like weather. Overnight, a brutal nor'easter struck, plunging temperatures and transforming heavy rain into a blinding blizzard. Hurricane-force winds whipped snow into towering drifts up to 50 feet high, burying houses, trains, and entire city blocks. Tens of thousands of passengers were trapped on elevated trains, where resourceful locals charged a quarter to help people down using makeshift ladders. The freezing chaos cut off communication, grounded shipping, and killed over 200 people in New York City alone. Former senator Roscoe Conkling famously perished after rejecting an expensive cab and walking through the deadly storm. Ultimately, the catastrophe paralyzed the modern metropolis and forced city leaders to drive overhead wires and transit systems permanently underground.

## Disputed or left out

- Exact snow depth totals within New York City vary slightly between accounts (some mention nearly two feet while others note 21 to 22 inches, with higher totals found in drifts and upstate regions; scripts should specify 'nearly two feet' or attribute specific measurements to regional totals).
- Total death toll figures differ across sources, ranging from 200 in New York City alone to over 400 across the entire Northeast; scripts should specify 'at least 200 in New York City' or attribute total regional counts accordingly.
