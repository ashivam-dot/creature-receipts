# ep017 research: Why Great White Sharks Rarely Eat Humans (Animal Myths, Busted)

Researched 2026-10-01 by the cloud studio (gemini-3.5-flash-lite, gemma-4-31b-it) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: For decades, movies like Jaws cemented the great white shark as a relentless man-eater hunting humans in the dark. But according to shark researchers, reality is far more surprising. Great white sharks do not normally prey on humans. Scientists explain that most recorded bites are actually curiosity or exploratory investigations. When a great white encounters an unfamiliar object, like a surfboard or a swimmer in a wetsuit, it uses its sensitive, highly flexible teeth like hands to take a test bite and figure out what it is. Because humans are too bony and lack the high-fat blubber of their preferred prey like seals, the sharks almost always spit us out rather than consume us. While they possess immense bite force and a terrifying reputation, encounters rarely result in fatalities, proving that humans are simply not on the menu.

## Disputed or left out

- Whether the Jersey Shore shark attacks of 1916 were caused by a great white shark or a bull shark; scripts should attribute the great white claim or leave it out.
- The exact maximum size and length records of great white sharks due to historical exaggerations and measurement difficulties; scripts should stick to reliably measured maximums around 6 meters.
