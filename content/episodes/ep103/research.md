# ep103 research: Why Did Hundreds Watch the Burning Ship in Halifax? 😳 (The Last Hours)

Researched 2026-10-07 by the cloud studio (gemini-3.7-flash) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: On the morning of December 6, 1917, the port of Halifax was a bustling wartime hub. In a narrow strait called 'The Narrows,' the SS Imo collided with the SS Mont-Blanc, a French freighter secretly packed with nearly 3,000 tons of high explosives. The impact was minor, but it ignited barrels of fuel on the deck. As the Mont-Blanc's crew fled in lifeboats, the abandoned ship drifted toward the city's busy docks. Unaware of the danger, thousands of residents—including children—pressed their faces against windows to watch the spectacular fire. At 9:04 AM, the ship detonated in the largest man-made explosion before the atomic bomb. The blast leveled the Richmond district, triggered an 18-meter tsunami, and hurled the ship’s anchor four kilometers. Over 2,000 died and 9,000 were injured, many permanently blinded by the windows they had been peering through just seconds before.

## Disputed or left out

- Whether Vince Coleman's message actually stopped the overnight train from Saint John is noted as 'uncertain' in S1 and S3.
- The number of blinded victims: S2 states 200 were blinded, S3 states 300, and S1 mentions 'Hundreds'.
- Responsibility for the collision: The initial inquiry blamed the Mont-Blanc solely, while later appeals split blame equally between both ships.
- Hearing the explosion: S3 mentions a crew in Massachusetts claimed to hear it, but S6 notes that 'tidal wave' stories from miles away are false.
