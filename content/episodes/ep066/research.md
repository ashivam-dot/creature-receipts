# ep066 research: How the Knights of Malta Defied an EMPIRE 🛡️ (The Last Hours)

Researched 2026-10-05 by the cloud studio (gemini-3.5-flash, gemini-3.5-flash-lite) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: In May 1565, a massive Ottoman armada carrying up to 40,000 soldiers descended on the rocky island of Malta, determined to wipe the 500 Knights of St. John off the earth. Led by the seventy-year-old Grand Master Jean de Valette, the defenders poisoned their own wells, harvested unripened crops, and prepared for total war. The Ottomans first targeted Fort St. Elmo, expecting a quick victory, but the small garrison held out for a month, costing the invaders thousands of lives and their famous admiral, Dragut. When the fort finally fell, the Ottomans floated decapitated knights on mock crucifixes across the harbor. In ruthless retaliation, Valette beheaded his Turkish prisoners and fired their heads back from cannons. Against impossible odds, Senglea and Birgu held through months of horrific bombardment until a Sicilian relief fleet finally arrived in September, forcing the shattered Ottoman army to retreat.

## Disputed or left out

- The exact size of the Ottoman army (Balbi di Correggio lists 48,000 invaders, whereas contemporary letters and Turkish registers suggest a lower count of 23,000 to 30,000; the script should present these varying estimates).
- The exact cause of Dragut's death (one account claims he was killed by stone splinters from a cannonball fired too low by his own gunners, while Bosio claims it was a lucky shot from Fort St. Angelo; the script should state it was from a stray shot or artillery fire and attribute the accounts).
- The August 20 climax involving a mine explosion and de Valette's counter-charge (Bradford describes a mine exploding and de Valette leading a desperate charge, but Balbi merely states de Valette ran to a threatened post, and Bosio mentions no mine detonation but rather a friendly fire panic; the script should focus on de Valette's personal leadership at the walls rather than a specific mine explosion).
