# ep025 R101 correction receipts — 2026-10-03

The published version (`qtmFWWw0npA`) was withdrawn to private. Its seventh beat falsely said all survivors were engineers in external engine cars. Its opening and crash account also assigned a more certain cause than the evidence supports. The original render log and favorable review applied to that withdrawn version; they are **not** a review of this replacement.

The original `research.json` retains its historical source list, while the replacement `short.yaml` points to the directly checked Trust and archival routes below.

## Direct source checks

| Corrected point | Source receipt |
|---|---|
| Six eventual survivors; four engineers escaped engine cars, while foreman engineer Harry Leech and wireless operator Arthur Disley escaped from inside the hull | [Airship Heritage Trust, R.101](https://airshipsonline.com/airships/r-101/), “The Survivors” and crash narrative. The Trust names Bell, Binks, Cook and Savory in external engine cars, Leech in the smoking room, and Disley in crew quarters. [GENUKI survivor list](https://www.genuki.org.uk/big/Indexes/R101Survivors), explicitly supplied by Harry Wykes of the Trust, lists the six. [Trust: Leech](https://airshipsonline.com/people/harry-leech/) says he rescued Disley; [Trust: Disley](https://airshipsonline.com/people/arthur-disley/) confirms his escape. |
| Forty-eight dead, six eventual survivors | [Airship Heritage Trust, R.101](https://airshipsonline.com/airships/r-101/) says seven men initially escaped the wreck; rigger Samuel Church died of his injuries three days later. The final count is six survivors. |
| Final trial and absent full-speed test | [Airship Heritage Trust, R.101](https://airshipsonline.com/airships/r-101/), “Final Trial Flight,” says the post-refit flight returned after just over 17 hours, and an engine cooler fault prevented a full-speed trial. It also records that the longer trial was curtailed. |
| Departure, weather, dives, impact, fire | [Airship Heritage Trust, R.101](https://airshipsonline.com/airships/r-101/), “Final Flight,” records the 4 October departure for India, rain and wind, two dives near Beauvais, ground contact, then fire. The page reports an official inquiry and discusses competing explanations. [Trust copy of the March 1931 public inquiry](https://airshipsonline.com/library/r-101-public-inquiry-document/) is an archival route to the formal evidence. [UK National Archives inquiry catalogue](https://discovery.nationalarchives.gov.uk/details/r/C5317784) identifies the inquiry papers; its page did not return readable text in this check. |

The Trust's own page describes a possible control-cable explanation, but that is presented as later research; the replacement avoids declaring it the established cause. It also avoids claiming that political pressure or gales alone *forced* the crash. The first dive's precise trigger remains disputed. The fire followed impact; no particular ignition source is asserted.

## Visual and script decisions

- Beat 1 opens on the archival wreckage photograph [R101 wreckage.jpg](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:R101_wreckage.jpg), with “SIX SURVIVED.” This brings the stakes into the first seconds. The standalone 4.2-second date card from the withdrawn version is removed.
- Beats 2, 3 and 4 use archived R101 photos near its Cardington mooring mast (1929) and its framework. The mast photo represents the craft, **not** the final flight in France. Source credits remain in `visuals.json` and `short.yaml`.
- Beat 6 uses a designed “4 + 2” card to make the correction unambiguous. It depicts no unsourced person or scene.
- The replacement title and ending no longer promise a settled answer to “Why did it crash?” Its 81-word script states the observed sequence and the surviving uncertainty.
- `withdrawal.json` remains the hold record. A new render and independent visual and speech review are required before any replacement upload; this correction does not clear the hold.
