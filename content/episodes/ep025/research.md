# ep025 research: Why Did Britain's Giant R101 Airship Crash? ⚠️ (Warnings Ignored)

Researched 2026-10-01 by the cloud studio (gemini-3.5-flash, gemini-3.5-flash-lite, gemini-3.6-flash, gemma-4-31b-it) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: In October 1930, Great Britain launched the R101, the world's largest flying craft, on a maiden overseas flight from Cardington to India. Designed by the British government to showcase imperial aviation, the giant hydrogen-filled airship suffered from excessive weight, structural leaks, and untested design changes. Despite missing full-speed trials and facing severe weather warnings, political pressure pushed the ship to depart on schedule with Secretary of State for Air Lord Thomson on board. Flying into heavy rain and strong gales over northern France, the airship plunged into two steep dives near Beauvais before slamming into a hillside. The impact ignited its hydrogen gas, creating a fierce inferno. Out of 54 people on board, 48 perished, including Lord Thomson and the airship's chief engineers. The tragedy permanently ended Britain's airship development program, and its sister ship, the R100, was scrapped soon after.

- The exact cause of the ignition after impact was not definitively proven; scripts should present the fire as occurring immediately upon impact without stating a specific spark source as settled fact.
