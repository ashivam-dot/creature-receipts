# ep043 source receipt — Little Bighorn

Checked directly on 2026-10-03 against the National Park Service (Little Bighorn Battlefield National Monument) and the Library of Congress. The numbered claims below match `research.json` and the citations in `script.json`. Quoted fragments are from the linked pages; bracketed explanations are editorial. NPS pages are several pages from one institution, not independent corroboration. The LOC account supplies a second institutional account for the vision and the destruction of Custer's battalion.

## Corrections and exclusions

- The old title, description, and beats 3–4 said Custer ignored scouts who warned that the village was too large. The checked NPS/LOC passages establish discovery and size of the village, but do not substantiate that specific warning or refusal. Those lines have been removed.
- The old research said neither Reno nor Benteen moved to reinforce Custer. NPS instead records Weir's northward movement, Benteen following, Reno's force attempting to follow, and their withdrawal under pressure from Lakota and Cheyenne warriors.
- The old beat 7 said more than 200 men were killed “in an hour.” The [NPS chronology](https://www.nps.gov/libi/learn/a-chronology-of-the-battle-of-the-little-bighorn.htm) places the battle in the afternoon and evening of June 25 and says it ended on June 26. It gives no one-hour window for the loss of Custer's battalion. The duration claim has been removed.
- The old description called the whole 7th Cavalry “annihilated”; Reno and Benteen's battalions survived. The revised description specifies Custer's battalion.
- The Benteen note was penned by adjutant W. W. Cooke on Custer's instruction. The new quote card credits Cooke and does not claim to show the original handwritten note or an aftermath telegram.
- The old Crook portrait was removed from the spoken sequence. Crook withdrew after the Rosebud according to the NPS chronology, but the checked passages do not establish what Custer knew of that withdrawal.
- The revised hook describes a reported vision. The script does not claim it caused Custer's decision or proves foreknowledge.
- The original `topic.json` is the historical assignment prompt and calls this the largest Native American encampment ever on the plains. The checked sources support a large village, not that superlative. `research.json` now uses a neutral topic description; the original assignment record is preserved as provenance.
