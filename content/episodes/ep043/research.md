# ep043 research: Why Did Custer Ignore the Warnings at Little Bighorn? ⚠️ (The Last Hours)

Researched 2026-10-02 by the cloud studio (gemini-3.5-flash, gemini-3.5-flash-lite) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: In June 1876, Lieutenant Colonel George Armstrong Custer marched his 7th Cavalry into the Montana Territory. Driven by gold discoveries in the sacred Black Hills, the U.S. government sought to force the Lakota and Cheyenne onto reservations. Weeks prior, spiritual leader Sitting Bull had envisioned U.S. soldiers falling like grasshoppers into his camp. Unaware that a supporting column under General Crook had already been forced to retreat at the Rosebud River, Custer ignored his Crow scouts' warnings of an immense, unprecedented village. Instead of waiting, Custer split his forces to prevent the Native Americans from scattering. Major Marcus Reno charged first but was quickly overwhelmed, fleeing in a chaotic, bloody rout to the bluffs. Left entirely isolated, Custer and his five companies were swiftly surrounded. Armed with repeating rifles, the warriors annihilated Custer’s immediate command of approximately 210 men in under an hour, leaving Custer dead with two bullet wounds.

## Disputed or left out

- Whether Custer attempted to cross the river at Minneconjou Ford (Medicine Tail Ford) is highly disputed by different historical witnesses. A script should attribute this, noting that some Native scouts claimed he tried to cross and was driven back, while others, including Chief Gall, stated he never reached the water.
- Whether Custer directly disobeyed orders is treated inconsistently across sources. A script should handle this neutrally by explaining that while some sources accuse him of disobeying, his official orders did grant him the flexibility to depart from the plan based on local conditions.
